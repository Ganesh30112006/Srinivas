Good morning everyone. Today we continue with CPU scheduling. The scheduler decides which process in the ready queue gets the processor next. The simplest policy is first come first served. It is easy to implement with a queue, but it suffers from the convoy effect: one long process makes every short process wait behind it, and the average waiting time grows. Shortest job first fixes this by running the process with the smallest next CPU burst. It gives the minimum average waiting time, but we cannot know the length of the next burst in advance, so in practice we predict it with an exponential average of previous bursts. Round robin is designed for time sharing systems. Each process gets a small unit of time called the time quantum, usually between ten and one hundred milliseconds. When the quantum expires, the process is preempted and placed at the end of the ready queue. If the quantum is very large, round robin behaves like first come first served. If it is very small, the system spends most of its time on context switches. Finally, multilevel feedback queues let a process move between queues, so interactive jobs stay responsive while long jobs still make progress.
