Today's topic is normalization in relational databases. The goal is to remove redundancy, because redundant data leads to update anomalies. If the same address is stored in many rows, changing it in one row but not the others leaves the database inconsistent. First normal form requires that every attribute holds a single atomic value, so there are no repeating groups. Second normal form requires that every non key attribute depends on the whole primary key, not on part of it. This matters only when the key is composite. Third normal form goes further: no non key attribute may depend on another non key attribute. Such a dependency is called a transitive dependency. Boyce Codd normal form is a stricter version of the third, where the left side of every functional dependency must be a super key. To normalize a table, we decompose it into smaller tables. A good decomposition must be lossless, which means joining the pieces gives back exactly the original table, and ideally it should also preserve dependencies. In practice, designers sometimes denormalize on purpose to make frequent queries faster, accepting some redundancy in exchange for fewer joins.
