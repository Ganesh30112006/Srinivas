Good morning everyone. Today we start CPU scheduling. The simplest algorithm is first come first served. It is easy to implement, but it suffers from the convoy effect: one long process makes every short process wait behind it, so the average waiting time grows.
[pause]
We measure a scheduler by its waiting time, its turnaround time and its response time. Response time matters most for interactive users. Shortest job first gives the lowest average waiting time, but it needs to know the length of the next CPU burst. Round robin gives every process a time quantum of about ten to one hundred milliseconds. If the time quantum is too large, round robin behaves like first come first served. If it is too small, the processor spends most of its time on context switches. Finally, multilevel feedback queues move processes between queues, so interactive jobs stay fast and long jobs still finish.
